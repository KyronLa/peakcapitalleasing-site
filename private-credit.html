Overview

What is private credit?

Private credit is lending that happens outside the traditional bank system. A non-bank lender, funded by private investors, provides capital directly to a business. Borrowers are usually mid-sized companies that need a flexible structure, and loans are typically secured by the business or its assets.

Structures

How the main structures compare

Senior secured loanAsset-based lendingEquipment finance (Peak)
What it isA loan secured by all or most of the borrower's assetsA loan sized to specific assets, such as receivables or inventoryA lease or loan secured by the equipment the business uses
Who holds the collateralThe lender, through a first claimThe lender, against a borrowing baseThe lender, as owner or holder of the equipment
Typical borrowerOperating companies with established cash flowCompanies with working capital needsBusinesses that need equipment without tying up bank lines
Payment patternInterest and principal on a set scheduleRevolving, follows the asset baseFixed monthly payments, with seasonal options

Why invest

Why consider private credit

  • Income structured through contractual loan payments
  • Lending secured by specific assets or the business itself
  • Diversification across borrowers, industries and equipment types
  • Funding that complements relationship bank lending
Risks

What to understand before investing

Limited liquidity

Private loans are not traded daily. An investment can be hard to sell before it matures.

Borrower default

Borrowers can fall behind on payments, and collateral may not cover the full loss.

Limited transparency

Loans are not publicly rated, and information is shared on a more limited basis.

Concentration

Results depend more on a few borrowers when a portfolio is not diversified.
